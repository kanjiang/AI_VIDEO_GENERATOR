# Writing Rules

## GENERAL RULES

1. **Action verbs. No descriptive prose.**
   The camera records only what can be seen and heard. No "he feels," "she realizes," "memories flash," or "the atmosphere is tense." Use actions: HITS, EXITS, GRABS, LOOKS, WHISPERS.

2. **Brevity is talent.** If two words can do the job, use two words. Every extra line is another minute of film.

3. **Change only what was requested.** Make targeted edits. Do not improve neighboring lines.

4. **Hollywood format.**
   - Slug line: ALL CAPS, bold, left margin
   - Action: left margin, present tense, action verbs only
   - Character cue: ALL CAPS, indent ≈ 2.2" from margin (3168 DXA)
   - Parenthetical: indent ≈ 1.6" from margin (2304 DXA)
   - Dialogue: 1" left + 1.5" right (1440 / 2160 DXA)
   - Transition: right margin, ALL CAPS

5. **One version, not five.** When you propose a scene, give one version and one reason. If the user rejects it, ask a narrow binary question and then give the next single version.

6. **Slug-line format.** `INT./EXT. — LOCATION — TIME OF DAY`. Typical times: DAWN / DAY / EVENING / NIGHT. The slug line should stay in one language throughout the project.

## BAD vs GOOD

❌ "Gray dawn washes the mountain peaks pink. An ancient temple rises above the abyss."

✅ "EXT. MOUNTAINS — DAWN. Three figures race along the snow ridge."

---

❌ "The hero feels the heart tearing from the pain of loss. The eyes are full of sorrow."

✅ "The hero LOOKS at the mountain." The actor plays the feeling.

---

❌ "The teacher says with a mysterious smile and an ancient, knowing voice:"

✅ "TEACHER: 'You already know.'"

---

❌ "A brutal, blood-soaked battle unfolds as the hero fights for life, revealing determination."

✅ "The hero HITS. The enemy FALLS. The hero TURNS. The second enemy IS ALREADY THERE."

## CHARACTER VOICE

Every major character must have a **recognizable voice**. Read any line without the name attached. It should still be clear who is speaking.

Voice parameters:
- **Line length** — short or long.
- **Register** — elevated or colloquial.
- **Slang** — whether they use it, and what kind.
- **Self-positioning in speech** — do they justify themselves, confess, deflect?
- **Silence** — who stays quiet more than others? Silence is also characterization.
- **Patterns** — what phrases or verbal habits repeat?

A strong tactic is to give each character a **different form of address** for the same person. One uses the real name. Another uses a nickname. A third never addresses that person directly.

## RHYTHM AND PACE

- **Fight / chase** — short verb lines. One verb per line. A cutting rhythm.

  ```
  The hero HITS.
  The enemy FALLS.
  The hero RAISES the sword.
  ```

- **Dialogue** — keep lines short. Use actions for pauses: "The hero stays silent," not "the hero says nothing because the hero does not know what to say."

- **Descriptive scenes** — still work through action, not abstract description.

  ❌ "The atmosphere became more and more tense."
  ✅ "The light goes out. Only breathing remains."

---

## AVOID

- **Internal monologue.** If it can be shown through action, show it.
- **Landscape description longer than one line.** One shot, one line.
- **Mood adjectives** such as ominous, anxious, mysterious. That is the viewer's reaction, not the writer's job.
- **Direct exposition through dialogue** such as "as you remember, we are close relatives and our mother was killed in 1992." That is an info dump.
- **Dialogue that repeats what is already visible in the frame.** If a character is crying, they do not need to say, "I am sad."

---

## SUPPORT

- **Objects as mines.** Every important object should move the plot. If a gun appears in act one, it must fire in act three. If it appears twice, the third appearance must pay it off.

- **Repeated lines.** The same line spoken in different contexts becomes a meaning-charged device. "I love you" at the start of the film and "I love you" before a killing are not the same line.

- **Silence.** A character's silence when the audience expects speech can be the loudest sound in the film.

- **Symmetry.** The first scene and the last scene should rhyme through an object, location, line, or action.
