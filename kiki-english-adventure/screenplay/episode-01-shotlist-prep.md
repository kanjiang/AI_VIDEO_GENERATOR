## Phase 1 — Script read

### Project form

- Series: `Kiki, Ready Set Go!`
- Episode: `Ep 01 — 加油加多啦 / Fill It Up!`
- Format: vertical preschool micro-episode.
- Aspect: `9:16`.
- Runtime target: about 75 seconds.
- Spoken language: English dialogue with limited Chinese support.
- Visual style: Pixar-grade Q-version 3D car animation; bright, warm, high readability.

### Emotional / camera register

- S1: energetic series identity; fast but clean vertical motion.
- S2: curiosity + problem statement; Kiki upper-center close medium, Bruno stable lower-background mass.
- S3: instructional clarity; Rosie gets clean teacher framing; pump prop repeats from three angles.
- S4: comic escalation; Kiki should occupy center vertical axis, rainbow oil arc rises upward through the frame.
- S5: correction and reward; movement calms, camera steadier, letter cloud rises above Kiki.
- S6: graphic clarity; text and faces must stay in safe zones.
- S7: one-second gag; fuel cap `dring-dring` must be readable instantly.

### Director beat

One focus: **the word contrast `Fill` vs `Feel` becomes a visible comic consequence**.

Pacing intent: the episode should feel like a tiny roller coaster:

1. location reveal,
2. phrase lesson,
3. wrong pronunciation,
4. messy payoff,
5. correct use,
6. audience repeat.

Pre-payoff relationship beat: Momo whispers the correction before Kiki says it correctly. This preserves the series theme that both loud and shy learners can help.

## Same-scene split risk

The Gas Station action will almost certainly be split into multiple prompts:

- S3: teaching phrase.
- S4A: wrong phrase and pump error.
- S4B: rainbow oil overflow.
- S5: correct phrase and reward.

Therefore every Gas Station segment must remount:

- `gas-station-exterior-scene.png`
- `gas-pump-closeup-prop.png` when pump/nozzle is active
- all visible character identity assets in the shot
- relevant FX reference only in segments where it appears

Do not drop Rosie/Pumpy/Momo from the background if they remain physically present. If not visible, explicitly say they are off-camera rather than inventing replacements.
